    Timber (EC5)¶

    The design of timber bars to EN 1995-1-1 (2004 or 2025), with Design ▸ Timber members (ULS). Before you start, see Materials and sections for design (the section must be of type Timber, with a grade, and a service class), Design codes and preferences for the parameters, and Buckling lengths.

    What is checked¶

    For every timber bar, for each selected ULS combination, the grouped cross-section and member verification of EN 1995-1-1 (from eurocodepy):

    • bending and axial force (N + My + Mz), including the compression stability factor kc and the lateral-torsional factor km;
    • shear (Vy, Vz) and their interaction;
    • torsion (T) and the shear and torsion interaction.

    The buckling lengths are obtained as for steel: collinear bars form a physical member, and its length is multiplied by the member's K factors (or the global defaults) to give l_0y, l_0z and l_0m. The forces are enveloped over each bar's critical points and over the combinations, and the sign of the axial force is kept, so both the tension and the compression points are checked (the rules differ).

    The resistances depend on the material, the service class and the duration of the load: the grade of the material (solid, glulam, LVL, CLT or panels) picks the partial factor γM from Code preferences, the service class of the section and the load-duration class of each load case give kmod and kdef. Both editions are implemented, chosen in Code preferences.

    In a plate model a grillage member has no axial force (its N slot is the torsion T) and the design checks bending, shear and torsion.

    Reading the results¶

    The Design panel lists, under Timber, one row per physical member:

    Column Meaning
    Member, Elements, Section, Grade, L the member, its bars, its section, its grade and length
    SC, Duration the service class and the load-duration class
    N_Ed, My_Ed, Mz_Ed, Vy_Ed, Vz_Ed the design actions
    k_cy, k_cz, k_m the stability factors
    N+M, Shear, Torsion the utilisation of each check
    Type, Taper, Apex M, Apex t90, Apex t90+V for tagged beams: see below
    Governing sections, Check where it governs, and the verdict

    On the canvas, the Timber design view colours each bar by its ratio.

    Special timber beams (pitched, tapered, curved)¶

    Design → Timber → Special timber beams opens the Beams panel in timber mode. Select the bars of a beam (they need not be collinear — a ridge or an arc is fine), press Assign to beam, and the design treats them as one member — or press Detect beams automatically… to have the chains of timber bars proposed (up to 45° from the horizontal, a ridge or an arc included; posts and columns are never proposed) and accept them. The list follows the beams: grouped, bottom to top and left to right, and along each beam from its left end. Draw the beam with the depth you want in each bar; nothing is generated, the design reads it:

    • the depth along the beam (a taper or a step), and which edge is inclined to the grain;
    • the ridge (apex) and, for an arc, its radius.

    The panel shows what was read and lets you override what cannot be read: the spacing of the lateral restraints, the lamination thickness, the inner radius, the type, the inclined edge and the depth change. These parameters are design-only, so the analysis results are kept; re-run Timber Members (ULS) to apply them. The table gains the Type, Taper (§6.4.2) and Apex (§6.4.3: bending, tension perpendicular to the grain, and its interaction with shear) columns. Use enough bars along a taper: a warning appears when the depth jumps between neighbouring bars by more than the Code-preferences limit.

    Below the table the elevation draws the beam as modelled, coloured by the utilisation at each position, with curves of N+M, shear, tapered edge and apex along its length. The calculation report of the member adds the geometry read from the bars and, step by step, each EN 1995-1-1 §6.4 check at the point where it governs. The apex clause (§6.4.3) applies to glued laminated timber and LVL only; for other products a warning is shown. Both code editions are implemented (EN 1995-1-1:2004 §6.4 and EN 1995-1-1:2025 §8.3.3–§8.3.4, chosen in Code preferences); the 2025 edition also notes when reinforcement against tension perpendicular to the grain is advised (optional). If you tick Apex reinforced for the beam, the unreinforced checks of the tension perpendicular to the grain and its interaction with shear do not apply (§8.3.4.2(1)); the result gives the force the reinforcement must carry (per metre of beam, and per element if you give the spacing a1). If you also give the bonded-on plane reinforcement (width, thickness, tensile and bond-line design strengths, and optionally the reinforced length and the number per element), its resistance is verified element by element (Eqs. 8.48–8.51). For screws and rods (fully threaded screws, rods with wood screw thread, bonded-in rods) give the diameter, inner diameter, steel strength; the partial factors γ_R = 1.30 and γ_M2 = 1.25 (Table 4.4) are used unless you give others: the resistance follows §11.2.2 with the reinforced length as withdrawal length. The spacing limits of §8.3.4.2(4) and the limits of Table 11.2 are reported as warnings.

    Bars that carry a beam tag are designed as tagged beams: the member is the whole tagged chain, and the buckling length is that of the span between supports, hinges and joining bars; a lateral spacing you set on the beam replaces K_LT·L.

    The step-by-step calculation of a member is in the design report.
