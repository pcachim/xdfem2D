    Design¶

    xdfem2D designs members and slabs to the Eurocodes, from the results of an analysis and the load combinations you choose. It is a design aid, not a substitute for engineering judgement: read Licenses & Disclaimer, which applies to every result the program produces, and the limits at the end of this page.

    Material Code Designs
    Concrete EN 1992-1-1 (EC2), 2004 or 2023 beams and grillage members (flexure, shear, torsion), the bars of each beam, columns, crack width, walls and slabs, punching
    Steel EN 1993-1-1 (EC3) bars: cross-section resistance and member stability
    Timber EN 1995-1-1 (EC5), 2004 or 2025 bars, including pitched, tapered and curved beams

    The formulae are those of eurocodepy, a required dependency; xdfem2D gathers the actions, calls the checks and shows the results.

    The Design menu¶

    Everything is in the Design menu, next to Analysis.

    Menu item Page
    Code preferences, Design combinations Design codes and preferences
    Buckling lengths… Buckling lengths
    Design report… Design reports
    Concrete ▸ Concrete members (ULS), Crack width, Suggest column reinforcement (ULS), Beam definitions, Beam bars detailing… Concrete: beams and columns
    Concrete ▸ Concrete areas (ULS) Concrete: slabs and walls
    Concrete ▸ Punching… Concrete: punching
    Steel ▸ Steel members (ULS) Steel (EC3)
    Timber ▸ Timber members (ULS), Special timber beams Timber (EC5)

    What a section needs in order to be designed (its type, its material and class, its cover, its profile) is on Materials and sections for design.

    Designing a model¶

    1. Model it with the right materials and sections. Set the type of each section and give its material a class, so that the strengths exist (Materials and sections for design).
    2. Analyse it (F5). The design reads the results of the analysis.
    3. Choose the combinations in Design ▸ Design combinations: the ULS tab for the designs, the SLS tab for the crack width. Generate the EN 1990 combinations with Analysis ▸ Auto Comb. (EN1990)… or define your own (Design codes and preferences).
    4. Check the code preferences in Design ▸ Code preferences: the edition, the partial factors and the parameters of each code.
    5. Run the design from its menu item. Each item designs for the combinations you chose, and shows its results.

    A design needs the elements to have the right properties, and skips an element it cannot design (see What is left out). If a member you expected is missing, look there first.

    Where the results are¶

    • The Design tables tab of the Results window has one tab per material: Concrete, with the sub-tabs Reinforcement, Shell reinf., Crack width, Columns, Beam bars, Suggested rebar and Punching; then Steel and Timber. Selecting a row in a table highlights that member on the canvas.
    • The canvas has design views, which colour the model by what was designed: the reinforcement envelopes, the shell reinforcement, the crack width, the utilisation of columns, steel and timber members. They are in the View list once there is something to show.
    • Design ▸ Design report… produces the step-by-step calculation of any check (Design reports).

    The results are stored with the model, in the .x2d, together with the design code they were produced with (see File Formats). They are discarded when the code preferences change or a new analysis replaces the results, because the ratios no longer apply.

    Limits¶

    • The model is 2D: each element has one in-plane bending moment. Concrete columns are checked for that moment about the major axis, with the minimum eccentricity of EC2 applied to the other; the out-of-plane bracing of steel and timber members is given by K_z (Buckling lengths).
    • Beams are designed as rectangular sections. Columns are rectangular or circular.
    • The only serviceability check is the crack width; there is no check of deflections.
    • With the 2023 edition of EC2, the crack width, the anchorage and lap lengths and the slenderness and N–M rules of columns keep the 2004 rules (Design codes and preferences).
    • Envelope combinations cannot be used for the design of slabs and walls, and the reaction-based punching force needs plain combinations too.
    • Punching is for plate models, and for the columns you define.
    • A national annex is chosen in a script, not in the dialog.
