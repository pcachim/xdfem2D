    Concrete: beams and columns¶

    The reinforced-concrete design of bars to EN 1992-1-1 (EC2). Slabs and walls are on Concrete: slabs and walls, and punching on Concrete: punching. Before you start, see Materials and sections for design and Design codes and preferences.

    Menu item What it does
    Design ▸ Concrete members (ULS) the reinforcement of every concrete bar: flexure, shear and, in a grillage, torsion; then the bars of each beam
    Design ▸ Crack width the crack-width check (SLS), from the reinforcement of the previous design
    Design ▸ Suggest column reinforcement (ULS) the lightest symmetric layout of bars for each column
    Design ▸ Beam definitions which bars form one continuous beam
    Design ▸ Beam bars detailing… the window where you refine the bars of each beam

    Beams and grillage members¶

    Concrete members (ULS) designs every bar whose section is of type Concrete. Each bar is checked at its critical sections: both ends, and the points of extreme bending moment and shear along the span, for every selected ULS combination. The section that needs the most steel is marked governing (★).

    A bar that is a column is not designed here: it goes to the column check (see Columns). Envelope combinations (an envelope, a seismic one) are designed by their maximum and minimum bands, each as a case of its own.

    Method, EN 1992-1-1:2004¶

    Flexure. Rectangular sections are designed for the dimensionless moment μ = M_Ed / (b·d²·fcd), giving the tension steel As; when μ exceeds the singly-reinforced limit, compression steel is added. The effective depth is d = h − cover. Minimum and maximum steel ratios follow EC2 §9.2.1.

    Shear. The concrete resistance VRd,c (EC2 §6.2.2) is checked first. When the shear exceeds it, the variable-angle truss model (§6.2.3) is used: the strut angle is swept from cot θ = 2.5 (θ ≈ 21.8°) down to cot θ = 1.0 (θ = 45°), choosing the flattest strut the concrete can carry so that the stirrup demand Asw/s is minimised, while respecting the strut-crushing limit VRd,max. If the section crushes even at θ = 45°, it is reported as inadequate in shear at the crushing limit.

    All the partial factors come from Code preferences: fcd = αcc·fck/γc and fyd = fyk/γs.

    Method, EN 1992-1-1:2023¶

    With the 2023 edition selected:

    • fcd = ηcc·ktc·fck/γc (Eq. 5.3), and αcc is not used. ktc is a preference.
    • Shear follows §8.2 (γv, ddg, τRdc,min, a compression field with ν = 0.5, the minimum stirrups of Eq. 12.4); the axial force and the shear span enter VRd,c.
    • Torsion follows §8.3 (ν from Annex G), and the combined shear and torsion Eq. 8.86.

    Each result row says which edition produced it. The crack width and the anchorage and lap lengths keep the 2004 rules in both editions.

    Grillage members¶

    In a plate model a bar is a grillage member: it bends out of its plane and carries St-Venant torsion, and the N slot of its end forces is the torsion T. The flexure and the shear are designed with N = 0, and the torsion to EC2 §6.3, combined with the shear (Eq. 6.29).

    Reading the results¶

    The Design panel lists, under Concrete ▸ Reinforcement, one row per critical section and combination:

    Column Meaning
    Element, Combination, Location which member, combination and section
    Reason why the section was checked (end, M⁺ or M⁻ peak, V peak)
    M_Ed, N_Ed, V_Ed design action effects at that section
    As,bot / As,top [cm²] required tension-face and compression-face steel
    Asw/s [cm²/m] required shear reinforcement (stirrup area per metre)
    cot θ strut inclination used by the variable-angle truss
    Strut the concrete strut crushing limit VRd,max
    Gov. ★ marks the governing section of each element

    On the canvas, the reinforcement envelopes are drawn along each member: the required longitudinal steel (As) and the shear reinforcement (Asw), enveloped over the selected combinations, station by station.

    Beam bars¶

    Right after the design, Concrete members (ULS) turns the required areas of each beam into bars, in the Beam bars tab: for each span, zone by zone, the bars of the bottom and top faces, the continuous ("through") bars, and the stirrups with their anchorage and lap lengths. The parameters are the EC2 · beam bars group of Code preferences.

    What a beam is. A beam is a continuous chain of spans: spans that meet collinearly at a support or at a column are joined into one beam (columns are ignored at the joint; a change of section, or a hinge, ends the beam). Beams are numbered B1, B2, … from bottom to top and from left to right. Beams the program finds are automatic. Design ▸ Beam definitions opens a panel where you select bars, tag them as one beam and give it a name, to correct what the detection would do. These tags are design data only: they do not change the analysis, so they can be edited on a locked model. Re-run the design to apply them.

    Zones. Each span is divided into zones, in one of two ways: at the cutoff points of the envelope (the required steel is quantised into a number of steps, and a zone boundary is placed wherever the envelope changes step; zones shorter than a minimum are merged), or at fixed fractions of the span. The requirement of a zone is the envelope of the steel required in it. The bars of each zone are chosen with the minimum steel As,min, uniform diameters, and the through bars present in every zone.

    Beam bars detailing… opens a separate window to refine the proposal, with the tabs Bars, Stirrups, Lengths and Parameters. You can split and merge zones, edit the bars and the stirrups with undo and redo, and the window checks each zone live against what is required. Zones you edit are manual and are kept when the design is re-run (flagged if they no longer comply); the others follow the new proposal. The decisions are stored with the model.

    Columns¶

    A bar is a column when its section is marked Physical column by default, or the bar itself is (a bar can override the section in Design ▸ Buckling lengths…, see Buckling lengths). Collinear bars are grouped into one physical member, and each is checked, over the selected combinations, for:

    • slenderness and second-order effects (EC2 §5.8), by the nominal curvature method (§5.8.8, the default) or the nominal stiffness method (§5.8.7), as the section says;
    • the N–M interaction of the section (§6.1), for the layout of bars the section gives: a number of bars for a circular section, or a number on each pair of faces for a rectangular one.

    The columns are rectangular or circular. xdfem2D is a 2D program, so each element has one in-plane bending moment, which is taken as the moment about the major axis; the moment about the other axis is zero, and the minimum-eccentricity rule of EC2 is still applied to it in the biaxial check. The checks are eurocodepy's (the 2023 edition uses the 2023 fcd and keeps the 2004 slenderness and N–M rules).

    The Columns tab lists, per member and combination, the slenderness (λy, λz), whether it is slender, the method, the moment resistances and the check. The canvas can colour the columns by their N–M utilisation.

    Design ▸ Suggest column reinforcement (ULS) does the reverse: instead of checking the layout you gave, it finds the lightest symmetric layout of bars (number and diameter) that satisfies EC2 for the governing combination of each column, in the Suggested rebar tab. A column for which no layout in the search range works is listed with the error, not dropped.

    Crack width¶

    Design ▸ Crack width checks the crack width (EN 1992-1-1 §7.3.4, always with the 2004 rules) of each concrete bar, with the reinforcement of the previous design, station by station (with an As,min floor), for the combinations ticked under SLS in Design combinations. It needs Concrete members (ULS) to have run first. Combined bending and axial force are accounted for. The limit w_max and the SLS combination type come from the preferences, and the main bar diameter from the section. The Crack width tab lists, per element and combination, the two ends and the governing station, whether the section is cracked, wk, the limit and the check; the canvas can draw the crack width along the member.

    The crack width is the only serviceability check: there is no check of deflections.

    The design report¶

    For any of these checks, Design ▸ Design report… produces the step-by-step calculation: see Design reports.
