    Plate models: slabs and grillages¶

    A plate model bends out of its plane: a slab (plate-bending triangles and quads) or a grillage (beams carrying transverse load and torsion). The nodes carry w, θx and θy. It is one of the two domains, chosen in New model (Slab / Grillage, out-of-plane). This page is the reference for it, in the same order as Plane models: the elements, the supports, the springs, the loads, the analyses, the results and design, the templates, and a script.

    What both domains share is on Modelling the structure, Supports, springs and constraints and Geometry objects.

    Line elements: grid members¶

    A grid member is a beam of a grillage: it carries bending and shear for the transverse load and St-Venant torsion. It reuses the bar section and gains a torsion constant J = β·b·h³ (with G = E/2(1+ν)). A hinge at an end releases the bending rotation; there is no torsion release. Its results are the M, V and T diagrams.

    Surface elements: slabs¶

    A slab is a surface that bends under transverse load. Its sections carry a thickness, a material and a formulation: the plate-bending element.

    Triangles¶

    Formulation Notes
    MITC3 (the default) Shear-deformable (Mindlin–Reissner): valid for thick slabs as well as thin ones, and reports a genuine shear field vx, vy. It converges to the exact Mindlin solution and passes the constant-moment patch test.
    DKT The thin-plate (Kirchhoff) element: no shear deformation, very accurate for thin slabs on coarse meshes.

    Use the default (MITC3) unless the slab is very thin and the mesh is coarse, where DKT converges with fewer elements. Both share the same nodes, DOFs, results and Wood–Armer design moments, so switching is just the section's formulation.

    Add triangles one by one (three node picks) on the Triangles sub-tab, or let a polygon geometry object mesh itself automatically.

    Quads¶

    A quad is the 4-node counterpart of the triangle. On a regular grid it avoids the diagonal bias that a triangle split introduces near supports and point loads; prefer quads on regular grids and keep triangles for irregular or concave outlines (the quad mesher is structured-only).

    Formulation Notes
    MITC4 (Mindlin, shear-locking-free) The recommended slab quad — the 4-node analogue of MITC3.
    DKT4 (thin, Kirchhoff) Four DKT triangles condensed to a quad; the thin-plate analogue of DKT.

    They carry the same moments and shears, Wood–Armer design and result views as the triangles, so a mixed tri/quad slab (quads in the regular field, triangles at an irregular edge) is one continuous model. Add quads one by one (four node picks, i → j → k → l counter-clockwise) from Structure ▸ Triangles ▸ Add quad, or let a rectangle/polygon object mesh into quads.

    Supports¶

    Supports restrain w, θx and θy. The canonical names are CLAMPED (all three), SIMPLE (w only), CLAMP-X / CLAMP-Y (w and one rotation), SYM-X / SYM-Y (one rotation: a symmetry line) and BLOCK. On the canvas the symbols read as slab edges: a simply-supported knife-edge (SIMPLE), a hatched clamp (CLAMPED), a supported edge with one rotation held (CLAMP-X/Y), and a dash-dot symmetry line (SYM-X/Y), which lets you model a whole slab from one wedge. The quick presets of the support dialog are SIMPLE, CLAMPED and SYM-X. The mechanism, the other tables and the on-canvas legend are on Supports, springs and constraints.

    Springs and foundations¶

    A node spring has kz (transverse) and ktx, kty (rotations). An element spring under a grid member resists the transverse deflection w only. A Winkler area spring kz [kN/m³] under a slab (a slab on grade) is lumped kz·A/3 at each triangle vertex and kz·A/4 at each quad vertex. It is a support, not a load, but it is applied over a region the same way as an area load. See Springs.

    Loads¶

    Load Applies to Parameters
    Point load a node Fz, Mx, My
    Distributed load a grid member transverse intensity qz
    Area pressure a slab region (a surface object, or a single triangle) pz [kN/m²]: the everyday slab load, drawn as a colour map
    Temperature a slab (a triangle, a quad or a surface); a grid member takes the same two values as a bar through-thickness gradient ΔT = T_top − T_bottom, which curves the plate (a uniform rise is stress-free)
    Support settlement a supported node prescribed w, θx, θy
    Nodal mass a node, for a Mass case Mx (the mass on w), My, Mtz (rotational inertias about x and y)

    A slab is loaded by area pressure (edge tractions are a plane-domain load). Everything about load cases, self-weight and the loads view is on Loads.

    Analysis¶

    Linear, NonLinear, Mass, Modal, Spectrum and Sequence are available. The mass sits on the transverse DOF w, so the modes are the out-of-plane (vertical) vibration and the response spectrum is vertical. P-Delta is not available: its geometric stiffness is built from in-plane axial force, which a plate model does not carry. See Analysis.

    Results and design¶

    • Grid members: M, V and T diagrams, deflection, reactions Rz, Mx, My.
    • Triangles and quads: the deflection field w, θx, θy, and the slab forces mx, my, mxy, m1, m2 (kNm/m) and vx, vy (kN/m). They are already stress resultants, so there is no force toggle.
    • Wood–Armer (W-A) design moments m*x,bot, m*y,bot, m*x,top, m*y,top, per element: the orthogonal-reinforcement moments an EC2 slab check consumes.
    • Design: the grillage members are designed as bars, with torsion (Concrete: beams and columns); the slab is designed for bending and out-of-plane shear (Concrete: slabs and walls), and checked for punching at its columns (Concrete: punching).

    See Results.

    Templates¶

    Slabs (rectangular slab, rib-stiffened, flat slab on columns, slab on grade, circular sector) and Grillages (beam grid, bridge deck) build plate models (see Templates).

    By script¶

    from xdfem2d import Structure2D

# A cantilever grillage member with a tip load (see examples/example-grillage-cantilever.py)
s = Structure2D(domain='plate')
s.add_material('C30', elastic_modulus=33e6, unit_weight=25.0, poisson=0.2)
s.add_section('BEAM', material_name='C30', b=0.3, h=0.5)     # grillage bar: gains J
s.add_node('N0', 0.0, 0.0)
s.add_node('N1', 3.0, 0.0)
s.add_bar_element('G1', node_i='N0', node_j='N1', section_name='BEAM')
s.add_support('CLAMPED', w=True, tx=True, ty=True)
s.assign_support('N0', 'CLAMPED')
s.add_load_case('LC', self_weight_factor=0.0)
s.add_point_load('N1', 'LC', fz=-10.0)                       # 10 kN down at the tip
r = s.calculate()
print(r['displacements']['LC']['N1'])                        # [w, θx, θy]

# For a slab: add_plate_section / add_quad_section, nodes, add_tri_element (3 nodes) or
# add_quad_element (4 nodes, counter-clockwise), supports, then the area pressure:
#   s.add_plate_section('SLAB', 'C30', thickness=0.20)         # MITC3; formulation='DKT' for thin plates
#   s.add_quad_section('SLABQ', 'C30', thickness=0.20, formulation='MITC4')   # DKT4 for thin
#   s.add_area_load('S1', 'LC', pz=-10.0)                      # a triangle OR a quad id (or a surface object)

    Runnable examples are in the examples/ folder: example-slab.py (thin slab with the DKT element, checked against the Kirchhoff Navier series), example-slab-mitc3.py (thick slab with MITC3, checked against the exact Mindlin series — the transverse-shear deflection the DKT misses), example-slab-quad.py, example-grillage.py (crossed two-beam grillage, load split) and example-grillage-cantilever.py (cantilever bar checked against the bending F·L³/3EI and the St-Venant torsion T·L/GJ).
